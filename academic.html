Recent Projects

The LGBT Acronym as Macro-Political and Micro-Interpersonal Synecdoche

LSA 2027 Oral Presentation

Co-authored with Liza Sulkin • Corpus-Assisted Discourse Studies (CADS) / (Auto)Ethnography

We investigate a 1-million-token corpus of private peer chats from mid-2010s to track how Russian-speaking teenagers use queer identity labels under Russia’s 2013 “gay propaganda” law.

Do Men Deserve Flowers? (...) Envy, and Desire in Contemporary Online Russian Discourse

LSA 2027 Oral Presentation

Single-Authored • Russian Digital Discourse / CADS

In this study, I analyze 27,300 comments under 54 Instagram Reels participating in an early 2026 viral trend that reacts to a male content creator receiving bouquets of red roses. I examine how Russian-speaking audiences enforce normative masculinity in online discourse, particularly by tracking the marked term muzhik (”real/masculine man”).

Pragmatics and Asymmetric Essentialization of Theyfab

LSA 2027 Oral Presentation

Co-authored with Sumeyye Nabieva • Trans Linguistics / Pragmatics in Discourse

In this study, we look at how birth-assignment labels become tools of intracommunity policing. Through a 17,519-token X corpus and a 1,608-token Reddit corpus, we analyze the change of pragmatic meaning of the term theyfab.

Vowel Reduction and Identity Indexicality in Diaspora Surzhyk

Master's Thesis (In Progress)

University of Massachusetts Boston • Sociophonetics & Language Contact

My Master’s thesis focuses on sociopolitical indexicality among post-2022 displaced Russophone Ukrainians in Alaska. In this mixed-methods sociophonetic project, I plan to observe whether Ukrainian diaspora speakers retain non-reduced vowel realizations when speaking Russian varieties, testing how vowel (non)reduction indexes post-2022 sociopolitical stance and regional identification.
